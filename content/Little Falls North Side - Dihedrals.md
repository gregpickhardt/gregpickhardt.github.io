## Dihedrals Left Side

### 189.  Janis Joplin  5.10 Sport**

5 bolts to the clip in anchors at the top.  The climb begins on a ledge about 12 ft off the ground that you scramble up to.  There is a bolt at the ledge to protect the belayer.  Perform balancy face moves at the bottom, then go through the overhang (actually one of the easiest parts of the climb).  Move to the left and try to get up the rounded off ledge to the clip-on anchors.

### 190.  Bobby McGee  5.9 Sport*

3 bolts to clip in anchors.  This route begins at same spot as Janis Joplin, but angles to the right.  More nice face moves.  To do this route in the prescribed manner you must honor a couple of restrictions.  A few feet up the route there is a large block on the ledge to the left; do not put your foot on it.  Also, stay on the face, do not slide right to go up the arête.  There is a hard to see hold on the face – I hope you find it.

### 191.  Unnamed

Climb the chimney to the top

### 192.  The Hisser 5.4 TR

A short distance uphill and left of Day Tripper.  Look for a series of flakes and right facing corners that lead to the top.

## Dihedrals Main Face

Historically most of the climbing has probably been done at the dihedrals, but since the train wreck it seems like less has been done there.  There are certainly some great climbs to do.  Some of the climbs have hangers at the top to make it easy to set up top ropes, including Day Tripper, Twin Cracks and Beast of Burden.

### 193.  Day Tripper  5.11b PG***

According to comments from climbers this is one of the best routes at Little Falls.  I have rarely seen it climbed, probably because it is so difficult.  The climb goes up the rounded arête defining the left edge of the dihedrals, primarily on the right side (the south face).  There are two starts for the first 10 ft, the “direct start” that goes up the south face, and another to the left that is on the curve of the arête or perhaps a little around the curve toward the west face.  The direct start is very difficult, the second a little easier.

### 194.  Twin Cracks  5.9 PG**

A Little Falls classic.  Sometimes it’s a crack climb, other times it’s a wide chimney.  Two overhangs to get past.  Use hand jambs, laybacks, stem, wedges, knee locks and other innovative moves.

### 195.  Unnamed

Under development

### 196.  Cotters Corner  5.6 R*

Most everybody’s first climb at the dihedrals; my first outdoor climb ever. May be 5.7 but the historical rating is 5.6.  To climb in the proper manner, get into the wide crack at the bottom, climb about 12 feet to get where the dihedral/corner begins, and then go straight up the corner crack.  Good balance is required.  There are at least two variations that make the climb a little easier.  At the start, instead of going up the crack move 6 or 8 ft to the right, climb up to the ledge and then drift back left to where the corner begins. The second is about two-thirds of the way up.  At a difficult spot in the corner, turn left onto a small ledge and tiptoe left out to arête.  Go 5 or 6 ft up the arête and then move back into the corner using some large blocks on the face.  This second alternate is wonderful fun with lots of exposure.  This climb is almost always top roped; there are trees at the top that make the setup easy.

### 197.  Unnamed

This route is still under development, so the rating is a guess.  A neat but difficult face climb.  Start like on Cotters Corner, or use the alternate Cotters Corner start off to the right.  At the ledge about 12 ft up, instead of going left into Cotters Corner just continue straight up the face, sliding to the right a little about half way up.

### 198.  Freight Train Blues  5.8 PG*

An excellent route that it seems like you can do over and over.  Except for the traverse on the ledge this route is pretty much continuous 5.8.  Stand up on a couple of big blocks and then use a layback to go over the overhang and grab the edge of the ledge above.  Mantle or otherwise get up onto the ledge, move left and then up about 6 ft.  Now go left to get around the next overhang (some tricky moves) and then move to the right and on up to the finish chimney.  For an easier start of this climb, use the Cotters Corner alternate start to get up to the ledge.  Usually top roped.  There are two trees at the top, but they are about 12 ft back from the edge so a static rope or long slings are useful for setting a top rope

### 199.  Desperado  5.11b X**

Follow the previous route. but instead of going left into the chimney climb directly through small overhangs and laybacks to a tiny ledge, then traverse right to a small left facing corner leading to the top.

### 200.  Coffin Crack (A3)

The brilliant, piton scared seamy crack going directly up the middle of the wall.  Where it comes to a T, a series of tweekers and laybacks lead to the exit on Desperado.  Still yearning to be free.

### 201.  Black Arête  5.12b X***

One of the beautiful arêtes at the dihedrals.  A very challenging route, one section is overhanging.  Generally stay on the south face, although just after starting you may want to swing around on the east face for a few feet.  It was marked to be bolted but didn’t get done.  The top anchors are in.  Hopefully to be finished in 2009.

### 202.  Devils Corner  5.11b PG***

A dihedral, crack, open book or whatever you want to call it.  Can be climbed with crack technique, by stemming with the feet up the sides of the book, using laybacks, or an infinitely variable combination of all three.  A demanding route however you do it.  You will need a static rope to connect a top rope to the trees just back from the top of the cliff.

### 203.  Satans Ceiling  5.10a R**

Stand about halfway between the two crack climbs (Devils Corner and The Devil won’t Care) and go right up the face.  The crux is getting over the first overhang about 20 ft up; use a layback off the edge at the left side of the overhang.  Continue up to the second overhang, lean way out, grab the monster flake above the overhang and try to work your feet up onto the flake. The easiest way to set up a top rope for this climb is by connecting a static rope to the trees just back from the top of the cliff.

### 204.  The Devil won’t Care  5.8 G**

Climb the wide crack a few feet to the right of Satans Ceiling.  Look for small footholds out on the face or inside the crack.  There is a strenuous mantle (or use good footwork) about halfway up to get on the ledge left of the crack. This climb is often led.  The same top rope setup used for Satans Ceiling also works on this climb.  Many top rope climbers will move to the left to do the spectacular Satans Ceiling second overhang when doing this route.

### 205.  Brittle Traverse  5.8 TR

Climb up a thin crack just right of the previous route.  Then about 15 ft off the ground traverse to the right.  And then?  According to Geoffrey Childs the route continues up the face, but the question is how to do so with 5.8 moves. We usually end up standing on top of the double arête and then sort of following the Beast of Burden route to the top.

### 206.  Beast of Burden  5.10b R

There is a somewhat dirty chimney just to the left of the Double Arête.  The junction of the left face of this chimney and the cliff face defines another arête, and this climb goes up it, generally staying a little on the cliff face side. Starting the climb, it is difficult to keep from barn dooring off to the right.

### 207.  The Juicer  5.13a Sport**

Originally named The Double Arête by the climbers that bolted it, but this name is more descriptive.  Four bolts to the clip in anchors.  Short and oh so challenging!  Work your way to the overhang at the bottom of the big protruding block of rock.  Reach up and place one hand on each of the vertical edges of this block (the two arêtes) and continue up.  Best done on a cool day, as there will probably be no opportunity to chalk up.  The last clip is hard to get in position for.  To practice on this climb, set up the top rope for Beast of Burden and redirect one strand of the rope through the rightmost clip-in at the top of Juicer.

Bill Griffith practices on The Juicer

### 208.  Blood on the Tracks  5.11c G**

A path leads through dense brush near the highest portion of the pale-colored cliff band right of the Dihedrals.  A few meters east of an obvious right -facing corner you will find a beautiful vertical crack.  Climb this (15') to a bulging wall where difficult face moves take you to the top.  Good climbing; frequently sieged.
