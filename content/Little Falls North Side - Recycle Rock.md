Recycle Rock is another of the cliffs on the large private land parcel.  It is hidden in the woods and not visible from the railroad tracks in the summer. Also, it is shaded and cooler in hot weather than the dihedrals or the wind wall.  You can walk along the tracks to the cliff from either direction.  From the east walk about 225 yards past the old stone factory to where there is a chest high rock shelf and then walk into the woods.  From the west start from underneath the Wind bridge and walk the tracks going about 135 yards past the edge of the cesspool pond to the rock shelf.  However, the easiest way to get to this cliff is to park in the small pullout up on Highway 5.  When you get out of your car you are right above the top of Recycle Rock.  However, it is not convenient to get down from here, so walk back west about 200 ft to where a path goes south.  This path drops down onto the old Dolgeville railroad right-of-way.  Walk back east on the right-of-way to the top of the cliff and either rappel down from one of the anchors or scramble down around the east side of the cliff.  There are some superb climbs here with possibilities for sport, trad or top roping.

### 93.  Jungle Gym  5.10a G*

At the far left end of the crag where it abuts the retaining wall a-perfect crack splits a three foot ceiling 25' off the ground.  Either climb the corner formed by the rock and retaining wall to a step out under the roof or climb directly (5.l0c TR) up the rock via two horizontal cracks and a few face holds.  The crack leads directly to the top.

### 94.  The Slant  5.10a PG

Right of the previous route a wide crack leads to a ledge with the stump of a bush on it.  It continues by diagonalling left across the slab above.  Harder and better than it looks.

### 95.  Tubby  5.9 PG**

Start on The Slant to get up to the ledge.  Then move to the right 3 or 4 feet using the small horizontal cracks for your hands and feet.  Using whatever footholds you can find, stand up and make a long reach to the good hold above (crux).  If you can’t make this move it is possible to continue up The Slant a little further and then step across back onto the route.  Continue angling slightly right and then at the top swing right and up to the clip in anchors.

### 96.  Fear and Loathing  5.11b Sport*

Five bolts to a clip in anchor.  The first of three finger killers put up here by Randall and Skibinski.  Start in a rounded concavity and mantle to a good crack five feet right of The Slant.  An odd combination of layback and face climbing moves around an edged depression will bring you to better holds and the lower angled slab above.  Trend right to gain an edge and then the top.

### 97.  Denizens of Delirium  5.11c TR*

Five feet right of Fear and Loathing climb somewhat more obvious edges into ever more dubious surroundings. Use whatever you can find to surmount the black face above.

### 98.  Doesn’t Matter  5.11a TR*

Oh. but it does.  A good route thought almost a variation of the previous line. Start off on good edges just to the right of Denizens of Delirium, then fight your way through a blank section at mid-height to reach some vertical seams above.

### 99. Blockhead Navigator  5.11a G**

A fine crack exercise, somewhat easy for the grade.  Climb out of the niche directly above the birch trees that hold the railing.  Make an awkward reach to a finger crack.  Tip-locks lead straight up to where a few dirty moves around a giant block will get you to the top.

### 100.  Unnamed (No information)

The vegetated off-width crack that ascends the left side of the gray face.

### 101.  Childs Play  5.10a** PG

The distinct crack splitting the middle right side of the gray face.  This climb can be started at the top of Stretch Marks or from the ground where Stokers Fix begins.  One bolt has been placed up high where the protection was thin so the protection rating may now be G.  On top of the cliff hangers have been placed so this climb can be top roped.  The hangers are set so 48 inch slings will just reach over the edge of the cliff.

### 102.  Mung Corner  5.9 PG

The left-facing corner at the right end of the gray face has a hand-sized and very awkward crack In it.

### 103. Stretch Marks  5.11a Sport**

Three bolts to anchors.  Climb the right side of the face, reaching around the right side of the arête for holds as needed.  Gets harder as you go up.  A delightful exercise in footwork.

### 104.  Stokers Fix  5.11a Bolted**

Five bolts to anchors.  Start in the corner and move up and right to the first bolt.  Continue up and then slightly back left and over the roof to the anchors. On top of the cliff there are hangers so this climb can be top roped.  The hangers are set so 48 inch slings will just reach over the edge of the cliff.  If you top rope the climb, the section above the regular anchors to the top of the cliff is 5.12.

Working on Stretch Marks to see where the bolts should go.  The Sure Thing is just left of Keegan Griffith (yellow T-shirt).

### 105.  The Sure Thing  5.8 G*

The historical rating of this climb is 5.8 although the start might be 5.9. Where a pair of cracks form a flat pillar climb up and left (10') to reach a comfortable pedestal.  Easy climbing takes you to the mid-height ledge. Angle to the right and head toward the anchors via one of the cracks.

### 106.  Psycho Cha-Cha  5.10d G*

Contrived but very worthwhile.  Start as if for The Sure Thing, but stay with the right hand crack.  Finger jam through a steep section to reach the sloping half-way ledge.  Bear slightly left from there to a distinct diagonal finger crack. Pass horizontal edges to reach a distinct indentation where the crack thins  to a seam.  A short set of tricky face mores at this point will take you directly to the top.  Copping out on the easier holds to the right will lower both the grade and your self-esteem.

### 107.  Dreamland  5.11b R **

This is the apparently blank face between Psycho Cha-Cha and Legitimate Beef.  Yep, sorry to say, Berzins led this one without top-roping or placing a bolt.  Brave boys and girls will begin by bouldering a few moves to reach a small ledge. Thin things will get you over the bulge and in worse trouble. Now reach left to place a blind-but-solid nut. Next, take a deep breath and puzzle out the frictiony moves (right or left) that will bring you to the large sloping ledge.  Continue straight up to the anchor shared with The Sure Thing and Legitimate Beef.

### 108.  Legitimate Beef  5.9- PG*

The first vertical crack right of the Dreamland face.  Better than it looks. Climb straight up to a tricky block, then on to the right side of the sloping ledge. Finish on any of the cracks that head to the shared anchor

### 109.  A Layback  5.6 TR

The very short layback crack behind a huge tree on the terrace above and right of Legitimate Beef.

### 110.  Darkest Argentina  5.7 G

Look for a thin finger crack splitting the short black slab uphill and 30' right of Legitimate Beef.
