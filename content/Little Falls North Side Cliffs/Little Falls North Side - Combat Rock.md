## South Vietnam

### 71.  Skin So Soft  5 .9 R

Find a 30' gray face just a few feet from the water's edge at the left end of the good rock. Climb the face just left of the edge. Don't blow the tricky protection placement at the crux.

### 72.  Funeral in Iran  5.10a R*

Locate a steep right-facing 30' slab with a small roof 10' off the ground and a horizontal crack a few feet above that. Climb up the right side of the face. mantling with care over the roof.  Better pro takes you back left.  Move to the middle and up at the top.

### 73.  Andrect  5.3 G

The big crack in the dirty open book.

### 74.  Slipped Disc  5.7 R

Almost a boulder problem.  Use the face left of a sharp arête with one hand on the edge.

### 75.  Cranial Mush  5.10b R

Another longish boulder problem: don't screw-up the first moves because there is no pro until the horizontal crack 12' off the ground.  Use the edge on your left and face holds on the right to get there, then go for easier ground.

### 76.  An Open and Closed Book  5.5 PG

The open book with a medley of dirty, awkward cracks.  Better than it looks, actually.

### 77.  Puzzled By Nature 5.10b R*

A short series of devious moves gets you to the small left-facing corner just right of the Book.  Mantle, then move up and slightly right climbing to a thin crack and the summit block.  Seriously run-out at the start.

### 78.  Cardiac Arête  5.11a PG*

Climb the arête and the face to its left. The bolt protects the lower moves but things can get a .little hairy just as the difficulties do ease off somewhat. Variations are

V1  Blur Hindu  5.7 PG  Climb the reverse side of the arête, relying somewhat on the face to the right. Better protection than it looks and better climbing

V2  The Poodle Bites  5.8 PG*  Take the obvious hand crack and continue straight up

### 79.  Cotton Tail  5.3 G

The big crack in the back of the corner.  Watch out for loose rocks!

### 80.  Brain Maggot Margin  5.10d/5.11d TR

A shallow left-facing corner (bear left of the start for Hippity-Hop) leads to a couple of tricky moves: left across the orange face on edges and a horizontal seam.  Make a difficult mantle, then a few easier moves lead to odd combinations and some loose surface rock which places you below a bulging roof.  Reach for a good ledge that presents you with the very awkward "Mickey Mantle.”  Not using the tree increases the standard a full grade.

### 81.  Hippity Hop  5.7+ G*

A couple of tricky moves off the ground will get you to a stump and the start of a beautiful hand crack. That's the hard part. The upper section is a cruise.

### 82.  Big-Headed Boy  5.9- R

The crux is difficult to protect until you are past it!  Follow an obvious vertical crack/seam to a block and a small roof.  A shallow horizontal crack and better holds above lead to an unnerving mantle.  Move up and left to the crack on the left side of the block above.

### 83.  Protein Turbo-Charge  5.9 R

Just right of Big-Headed Boy a shallow left-leaning grove leads to a move right around a bulge, then a mantle, a vertical crack and small ledges in a straight line to the top.

### 84.  Spanking Daddy (Instead)  5.10? PG

Why not?  Start by bouldering a move (V1) off the ground just right of Protein Turbo-Charge: use the horizontal crack left of a small tree.  It gets much easier above.

### 85.  Industrial Zouk  5.10c PG*

A good route, a little slim at the start but well-protected (bolt) at the crux. Climb the center of the somewhat concave 30' slab located between two chimneys. The start is a little touchy: good holds in an awkward combination will bring you to a horizontal crack. A vertical finger slot takes a #2 TCU and a couple easier moves will let you clip the bolt.

### 86.  Chieu Hoi  5 .2 PG

Climb the chimney on the right.

### 87.  Bunny Trail  5.7 G

Another long boulder problem which requires same honest jamming.  An obvious, slightly diagonalling finger crack at the right end of the crag.

## North Vietnam

### 88.  Joes Corner  5.5 G

Something different: a route that climbs a corner formed on the right by rock and on the left by quarry blocks.

### 89.  Paternity Nerves  5.11a PG/R*

A few feet right of Joes Corner flakes lead to a thin horizontal.  Then follow a thin vertical crack.  Once you’ve bypassed the wide horizontal slot about mid-height the crux is the unprotected slab above.  Nervous fathers can step right to avoid that bit.  The nervous father was Bill Griffith; his son Keegan can be seen in the Recycle Rock photo.

### 90.  Ma Bell Calling  5.9 G

Ascend the obvious parallel finger-cracks to a slot.  Scramble to the top.

### 91.  Arthur Murray Two-Step  5.8 PG

A good start in search of a stronger finish.  Locate a left-facing bulge behind a big dead tree 10' right of Ma Bell.  Work up to a fun move over the block above.  Diagonal left to finish.
