The Wind Wall is either named for the former mayor of Little Falls, or it is named for the bridge it nearly sits under (which in turn is named for the mayor).  Ted Wind was mayor of Little Falls for 33 years, retiring at the end of 2007.  This south facing crag sits on the southern edge of Highway 5, a couple hundred feet west of where the Wind bridge tees into the highway.  If you walk around to the back of the cliff you will actually walk on the edge of highway 5.  The cliff itself and the flat area at the base of the Wind Wall is on the privately owned parcel discussed in the introduction.  The flat area above the cliffs is part of the highway right-of-way (ROW).

To get to the cliff, from the north end of the Wind Bridge turn left (west) on Highway 5 and drive a few hundred feet to the stoplight.  Turn left on River Road, which curves around left and heads east, right past the Wind Wall. Please do not park at the base of the cliffs; it is best to be inconspicuous. Drive on past and park underneath the bridge a few hundred feet further on. Walk back up the road.  Below the west end of the cliff there is a path from the road up to the wall.

To set up top ropes without doing any climbs, go to the west end of the wall and scramble up to the highway.  Walk along the shoulder back to the east for about 100 ft and then scramble up to the top of the cliff on some easy step like features.  The original guide divided the Wind Wall into west and east sections; even though it is a small cliff I will continue with that format.

## West End of the Wind Wall

### 1.  Local Hero  5.10b G

An off-width hand and fist crack leading to a fingery finish.

### 2.  T&A  5.11d TR*

Stands for tendonitis and arthritis, both of which your fingers will experience on this short but demanding problem. Climb a perplexing sequence of downpulls and sidecuts on a choppy face to reach an incipient crack that turns into a shallow corner. This will get you to a disappointingly spare horizontal. ### 3.  Faces in the Crowd  5.10 TR

Find the narrow buttress in a shallow alcove with a small, moon-shaped overlap ten feet off the ground in a left-facing corner. This route -- almost a long boulder problem -- climbs the two small roofs.

### 4.  Alfred Overhang  5.11c/d PG***

A classic. The piton below the roof recalls a time when this route was used as an aid climb.  A strenuous, constantly demanding adventure. Take the obvious line (often wet) to an awkward stance below the roof. Swing over and make a few more thoughtful moves to the belay ledge above. A pumper!

### 5.  Loose Footed  5.11b R**

Continuous and sometimes a bit wet. Climb the corner to the roof and then turn the roof on the right. Follow a system of easier cracks to the top.

### 6.  Aversion Streak  5.10a R

Instead of in the corner, start out on the arête and climb directly to the crack system part of Loose Footed.

### 7.  Wing Tips  5.10d PG*

The face right of Aversion Streak has an excellent route on its right side. Mantle horizontal cracks to reach a shallow right-facing corner. Climb up to reach another horizontal (look for the piton), then work slightly left and up to a pair of shallow cracks (sometimes wet) that lead to the top. Easy for the grade once the sequence is discovered.

### 8.  Recess Time  5.9+ R

Climb more or less directly up the center of a narrow buttress split by two obvious horizontal cracks. Short but excellent; thought provoking to lead.  A variation is

Var1  Boxer Shorts  5.10b TR  Climb along the right edge above the two horizontal cracks.

## East End Wind Wall

### 9.  Reserve

### 10.  Wind Crack  5.7 G*

Locate a series of small tight blocks in a vertical crack system. Follow this line straight to the top.

V1  Hard to Hold  5.10a G  Harder than it looks. Climb the main block-and-crack system but step left about half way up and reach into the narrow crack above. Awkward jams (that benefit smaller hands) lead to a very difficult mantle at the top.

V2  Pants in a Cloud  5.7 R  From the main crack system, move right to a series of shallow 'steps" on the face. diagonalling up and further right to reach a small roof and the top.

V3  Almost Like Magic  5.l0c TR  Almost its own separate route.  Face climb or traverse left on an undercling to reach some shallow right-facing edges on a scooped face. Work your way to ledges and finish on the previous route.

### 11.  Reserve

### 12.  The Wanderer  5.7 Sport

Just left of D'Vine Crack bolts meander up to left and then more straight up to clip-in anchors at top.  Route intended as a warm-up, easy way to get to top of cliff, and fun for less experienced climbers.  Originally this climb had no name, but this one appears to have become attached to it.

### 13.  D'Vine Crack  5 .7 G*

Named for its ability to sprout poison ivy.  Follow the slightly leaning crack system to the top. Good jamming technique is required.

### 14.  Squeeze Play  5.10c R**

Just right of D'Vine Crack is a narrow face with a distinct pair of squarish red slabs set out from the wall.-one above the other.  They mark the line of this demanding route. Stay between the two routes on either side, working your way past the red slabs, a pair of good horizontal cracks and some thin face climbing to reach a final vertical seam.  Carry a couple very small cams and a couple large ones; double ropes help.

### 15.  Digits or Aunt Smears  5.l0d/5.lla PG**

Another classic & terrific climbing in a small package.  Locate the thin, perfect crack above a flat boulder.  Climb to the "triangle," then work right and up to a hand slot, then back left to the final finger cracks.  Enjoyable and generally well-protected with the hardest moves near the ground.  The difficulty of the initial crack is relative to finger size.

### 16.  Fast on the Trigger  5.10b PG*

Work up a thin finger crack to painful jams.  A few feet higher and you can reach a ledge.  Take the shallow chimney to finish.

### 17.  The Mind Thing  5 9- PG

A long boulder problem.  Climb the crack system 20' right of Fast on the Trigger.  Ledges to either side offer an easy out.
