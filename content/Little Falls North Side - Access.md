## Introduction

The railroad track crags are located on the northern banks of the Mohawk River. Access here is questionable, as you must cross the railroad tracks to reach the crags. From the property records it is difficult if not impossible to determine who owns or controls access to some of the cliffs.  There is no written or oral agreement with the owner(s) allowing public use of the cliffs; you must obtain permission to climb.  You may be denied access to the cliffs at any time.  This guidebook in no way implies that you have a right to access to the cliffs or to climb on them.

Though many of the routes here can be top-roped, many take good protection and are best experienced on the lead. Hardware (bolts and belay anchors) exists on some of the railroad track cliffs. There is no certification that this hardware was properly installed or that it has not been tampered with.  In the past, hardware has been damaged or removed at Little Falls cliffs, and recently hardware at other climbing sites in New York has been damaged.  The inspection and use of any hardware is entirely your own responsibility.

There are numerous cliffs along the Mohawk River in the village of Little Falls. They exist because of a fortunate combination of mountain building, faulting, glaciers and the Mohawk River. Some of the cliffs are on Moss Island, “owned” by the NYS Canal Corporation (a division of the Thruway Authority). The Moss Island cliffs are covered in a separate guide.

On the north side of the Mohawk River, are a series of south facing cliffs from 30 to 70 ft high.  The formation at the western end, just across from Moss Island is the Wind Wall.  At the eastern end, about a mile downstream, is the Dihedrals.  Each of the other cliffs in the series is also named.  The map in Figure 1 on the proceeding page shows the general layout. The ownership of these cliffs is complicated.  There is a large privately owned parcel and many of the cliffs are clearly on this parcel; on others the cliff face seems to set right on the border between the private parcel and land owned by other organizations.  Some of the other organizations owning land are CSX Railroad, City of Little Falls and the State of New York.  The NYS owned land is mostly along the edge of highway 5, which runs east/west along the top of the cliffs.  Another complication is that some of the land is within the city limits of Little Falls, while other parts are in the Town of Manheim.  In the past climbers were much more likely to be tossed off cliffs within Little Falls than they were those in Manheim.  If you climb on any cliffs in the privately owned parcel you need the owners permission.

## Access

To get to the north side crags, for most people the easiest way is off the Thruway (I90) at Exit 29A, and north on highway 169.  As the highway angles left to go along the Mohawk River you can look across and see the Dihedrals and just west of them the old stone factory.  Continue on 169 past the turnoff for Moss Island and cross the river to the T at highway 5.  For the [[Little Falls North Side - Wind Wall|Wind Wall]] or for an approach to the other cliffs from the west, turn left onto highway 5, drive a few hundred feet and turn left again onto River Rd, which angles back east past the base of the Wind Wall.  It is best not to park in front of the wall (don’t attract attention).  Instead drive on down the slope and park under the bridge.  Walk back up River Rd to the west end of the wall, there is a path here.  To get to the other crags from this parking spot just walk east along the CSX tracks.

To access the cliffs from the east end, at the T intersection turn right on highway 5, go east past the highway 167 turnoff and then turn right on Bidleman Rd.  At the bottom of Bidleman Rd angle right onto River Rd, drive under the railroad bridge and turn into the parking lot on the right (north side) just a few hundred feet past the bridge.  From the parking lot walk north over the railroad tracks right where the signaling structure; you will be in front of the [[Little Falls North Side - Dihedrals|Dihedrals]].

There is a third access point.  When on Highway 5, instead of going all the way to Bidleman Rd, park in the small overlook lot on the right side of the highway, just a few hundred yards past the 167 turnoff.  This overlook is directly above Recycle Rock.  However, it best not to try to jump directly from the highway to the top of the cliff.  Instead, walk back west along highway 5 for about 60 m (200 ft) and turn left onto a path.  This path first goes right, then angles back left and drops to the old [[Little Falls North Side - Dolgeville Railroad ROW Wall|Dolgeville Railroad ROW]].  From here you can turn east to go to [[Little Falls North Side - Recycle Rock|Recycle Rock]] and the other eastern cliffs or turn right for the [[Little Falls North Side - Cesspool Pond|Cesspool Pond]] cliffs.
